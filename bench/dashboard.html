Six ways to handle a half-written answer

Time runs left to right on the same wall clock in every lane; nothing else is shared. Each lane follows one answer, which started on weights v, and the two processes behind it: the engine that generates and the trainer that updates. The weight update, when v+1 is copied into the engine, is the black segment on each lane's bars; it comes when the regime allows it: at once in the in-flight and abort regimes, after the running answers finish in the drain, and only after the batch and the trainer's turn in the synchronous regime, where the two take turns and the trainer has nothing to train on until the batch ends. Each token is coloured by the weights that sampled it, and the bar under it by the weights that computed its cached keys and values (KV), which every later token reads. Hover a cell for its word; the readout names the engine's phase at the cursor. A schematic of the mechanism; the measurements follow. The seventh regime, versioned LoRA adapters (F), needs adapters and belongs to the LoRA tier.

The first pilot from W0

A, D and E from the shared warm start W0, one seed each, ten minutes on two H100s, every snapshot scored on GSM8K test. Drag T: accuracy at T reads one point on each curve; the area (the primary endpoint) is the curve's mean height over the shaded span.

The synchronous learning curves

Arm A from the base model on each task, a snapshot every 20 to 25 steps scored greedily: where the warm start W0 sits and how long the climb lasts. On GSM8K the curve placed W0 at step 50 with 300 steps of climb left. On MATH it reached its plateau by step 40 with no settled snapshot left below it, which is why the pre-registered MATH grid did not run and the climb tier starts every arm at the base model.

How wrong is a stale KV cache?

Phase 1 teacher-forces answers through checkpoints k optimiser steps apart and compares three next-token distributions at each position after a swap. Drift is KL(OLD‖FRESH). R is how much of that drift a stale cache keeps: KL(FRESH‖STALE) / KL(FRESH‖OLD), as a ratio of means. At R = 1 a stale cache is as wrong as the old weights; at R = 0 it is harmless.

The noise floor

The engine samples; the trainer recomputes the log-probability of every sampled token from the very same weights. k3 estimates KL(engine‖trainer) per token. Swap conditions push identical weights mid-answer, so any change is the swap's doing; they force answers to full length, so they compare with the forced row.
